# THE HYDE STUDIO — Website Blueprint

**Purpose:** Turn the homepage into a clear introduction, a credible portfolio, and a reliable route to enquiries.  
**Launch model:** A strong homepage first; detailed case studies added as approved material is ready.

---

## 1. Website goals

### Primary goal

Help a potential client understand what Hyde can do and start a relevant project conversation.

### Supporting goals

- Build trust through genuine client work and clear explanations of Hyde’s role.
- Show that strategy, design, development, visual communication, and digital growth can work together within one small team.
- Make the team feel approachable and collaborative.
- Create a foundation for future learning resources and community activity without pretending those programmes already exist.

### Audience priorities

1. **Potential clients / founders:** need to understand capability, credibility, process, and how to get started.
2. **Growing businesses / teams:** need a capable partner who can take an idea from problem framing through execution.
3. **Students and emerging creatives:** may later engage with Hyde’s educational content, events, or workshops. This is a future audience, not the main conversion target for the initial website.

## 2. Recommended information architecture

For launch, use a single-page homepage with clear anchor navigation:

- **Home** — hero and positioning
- **Work** — client projects and selected experiments
- **Capabilities** — services Hyde currently offers
- **Studio** — team, working approach, and point of view
- **Contact** — enquiry form and direct contact details

Add these only when they contain real content:

- **Case studies** — individual project pages
- **Notes / Fieldnotes** — articles, learnings, process breakdowns
- **Hyde Sessions** — workshops, student learning, talks, or mentoring programmes

Do not add empty nav items or “Coming soon” pages just to make the site appear larger.

## 3. Homepage section plan

### 01 — Header

**Elements:** transparent or solid header, wordmark, Work / Capabilities / Studio / Contact links, one contact CTA.

**Behaviour:** sticky only if it remains compact and does not hide focused elements. On mobile, use a clear menu control with keyboard support.

### 02 — Hero

**Eyebrow**

`INDEPENDENT CREATIVE & PRODUCT STUDIO · OPERATING SINCE 2025`

**Headline option A**

`We turn ideas into things people can use.`

**Supporting copy**

`Hyde brings product thinking, design, development, and digital growth together — helping ideas move from the first conversation to a real experience.`

**Primary CTA:** `Start a conversation`  
**Secondary CTA:** `Explore our work`

**Visual direction:** oversized wordmark or bold typographic composition, black/white field, a restrained Volt marker. Avoid a generic glowing orb, stock photo hero, or carousel.

### 03 — Selected work

**Intro**

`A few things we’ve helped bring to life.`

Show 3–5 strongest approved projects at launch. Use real project imagery and a concise description. The card should communicate the product and Hyde’s contribution, not just display a decorative mockup.

**Suggested project card fields**

- Project name
- Client / industry (only when approved)
- Project type: client work / internal product / self-initiated concept
- Hyde’s role
- One-sentence context
- Cover image
- Case-study link or external product link

**Case-study content order**

1. The context and challenge
2. The people and needs involved
3. What Hyde was responsible for
4. The approach and key decisions
5. What was delivered
6. Outcome or learning (only verifiable claims)
7. Credits and collaborators, where appropriate

If a project is confidential, use an approved anonymised description and remove identifying details. Do not publish private client material without permission.

### 04 — Capabilities

Keep the offer understandable. A suggested launch set:

**Product & UX design**  
Research, user flows, information architecture, wireframes, interface design, prototyping, and design systems.

**Websites & digital products**  
Responsive websites, product interfaces, front-end implementation, and practical product experiences.

**Visual identity & communication**  
Brand identity, graphic design, campaign assets, and visual systems.

**Digital strategy & growth**  
Digital marketing, content direction, campaign support, and ways to improve reach and engagement.

Only show a capability if Hyde has the skills, capacity, and process to deliver it now. If backend engineering, research recruitment, analytics, or media buying are not currently available, do not imply that they are included by default.

### 05 — How we work

Use a simple four-step process:

1. **Understand** — align on the problem, audience, context, and constraints.
2. **Shape** — define the direction, scope, and experience.
3. **Make** — design, build, review, and refine together.
4. **Improve** — launch, learn from feedback, and identify the next useful step.

Keep this section grounded. It should explain how a client will experience working with Hyde, not present a fictional enterprise methodology.

### 06 — The studio

**Headline option**

`Small team. Different strengths. Shared ownership.`

**Suggested copy**

`We’re a multidisciplinary team bringing product design, development, visual design, and digital marketing into the same conversation. That helps us look at the whole experience — not just the part we happen to be making.`

Use real team portraits or a small candid team image if everyone agrees. Otherwise, use role-led descriptions or a behind-the-scenes image. Do not create fake employee profiles or titles.

### 07 — A point of view / future-facing section

Optional launch copy:

`Good work should leave people more capable.`

`We want to make useful products with clients today and make the process of building digital things easier to understand for the next generation of makers.`

This creates a bridge to Hyde’s longer-term education ambition without claiming that a school, academy, community, or workshop programme is already operating.

### 08 — Contact

**Headline**

`Have something worth building? Let’s talk.`

**Supporting copy**

`Tell us what you’re trying to solve, what stage you’re at, and what kind of help would move it forward.`

**Form fields**

- Name (required)
- Work email (required)
- Company / project name (optional)
- What do you need help with? (required)
- Project context (required, short message)
- Budget range (optional; include only if the team is ready to qualify enquiries this way)

**Submit label:** `Send project enquiry`

Provide an accessible confirmation state and a real delivery path (email service, CRM, or backend). Do not ship a form that only displays a success animation without sending the enquiry.

### 09 — Footer

- Wordmark
- One-line studio description
- Work / Capabilities / Studio / Contact links
- Email and approved social links
- Copyright year generated dynamically
- Required business/legal information

## 4. Work presentation: credibility without inflated claims

The portfolio is the proof section. Keep it honest and specific.

### Project categories

Use labels to distinguish:

- **Client work** — commissioned work for an external client.
- **Internal product** — a product Hyde is building or has built for itself.
- **Concept / experiment** — self-initiated exploration that is not represented as a shipped client product.

### Project evidence checklist

For each public project, confirm:

- Client permission to show the name and/or logo.
- Permission to publish screens, photographs, and product links.
- Accurate role attribution and collaborator credits.
- The scope Hyde actually delivered.
- Outcomes supported by evidence. Do not convert project activity into invented percentage improvements.
- A clear note where an app, website, or feature is not publicly available.

## 5. Visual and interaction direction

- Use the black wordmark on light surfaces and the white wordmark on dark surfaces.
- Let large typography, whitespace, and selected work lead the page.
- Alternate light and dark sections only when it improves pacing and hierarchy.
- Use Hyde Volt on the main CTA, active navigation, small status markers, and selected graphic details.
- Use screenshots at readable sizes. Avoid putting small product screens inside several layers of device mockups.
- Use one consistent project-card treatment and one consistent image ratio where possible.
- Provide a useful hover/focus state for all interactive elements. Hover must not be the only way to reveal information.
- No autoplay video with sound, cursor replacement, scroll hijacking, or motion that blocks reading.

## 6. Search and sharing essentials

- Page title: `The Hyde Studio — Product Design, Digital Experiences & Creative Work` (adjust to the confirmed offer).
- Meta description: `The Hyde Studio is an independent multidisciplinary team working across product design, websites, digital products, visual identity, and digital growth.`
- Use one descriptive H1 on the homepage.
- Give project images meaningful alt text; use empty alt text for decorative imagery.
- Add Open Graph title, description, and a branded share image.
- Add canonical URL once the domain is final.
- Add structured data only for facts that are verified and current.
- Create a custom 404 page that points visitors back to Work or Contact.

## 7. Future direction — grow beyond a service studio

The service work can be the first engine, while learning and community become a second, carefully developed part of Hyde. Build these in stages so the studio does not overpromise.

### Phase 1 — Establish proof (now)

- Ship the homepage and selected work.
- Make the service offer clear and narrow enough to deliver well.
- Document each project while the decisions are still fresh.
- Develop a repeatable project intake, proposal, review, and handoff process.
- Publish useful observations from real work, with client confidentiality respected.

### Phase 2 — Share the practice

- Publish short field notes: how briefs work, how designers collaborate with developers, how products move from idea to release, what client feedback changes, and what the team learned.
- Create practical downloadable resources, checklists, and templates.
- Host occasional open sessions or portfolio reviews only when the team can support them.
- Partner with colleges or communities where there is a genuine learning opportunity.

### Phase 3 — Build a learning community

- Test a small workshop series before creating a large programme.
- Focus on practical, real-world collaboration: briefs, teamwork, feedback, constraints, delivery, and communication.
- Give learners a clear outcome such as a documented project, presentation, or collaborative build.
- Decide whether the model is free, paid, sponsored, or partner-led after testing demand and operating cost.

### Phase 4 — Create repeatable products

Potential directions, to validate rather than promise:

- Reusable design and delivery toolkits.
- Small digital products that solve problems Hyde repeatedly encounters.
- Collaborative project programmes with student teams.
- A studio-led incubator for selected ideas, if there is a sustainable way to fund and support them.

The long-term story could be: **make useful things with clients, share what the team learns, and help more people understand how real products are made.**

## 8. What not to build yet

- A large multi-page corporate site with empty pages.
- A blog with no publishing cadence.
- A course platform before the learning format is tested.
- A complex animation system before the content and layout are right.
- A dashboard or client portal unless a real client workflow requires one.
- A list of every design tool the team has used.

## 9. Launch checklist

### Brand and content

- [ ] Confirm final hero copy and service names.
- [ ] Confirm the contact email and response owner.
- [ ] Approve the public team description.
- [ ] Select 3–5 projects and verify every claim, asset, and link.
- [ ] Add real client names/logos only with permission.

### UX and accessibility

- [ ] Check 360 px mobile, tablet, laptop, and wide desktop layouts.
- [ ] Test keyboard-only navigation and visible focus.
- [ ] Check normal-text contrast, button states, and disabled states.
- [ ] Test reduced-motion preferences.
- [ ] Test form labels, validation, success, failure, and delivery.
- [ ] Confirm heading order and meaningful alt text.

### Technical

- [ ] Add page metadata, social preview, and favicon.
- [ ] Optimise images and lazy-load below-the-fold media.
- [ ] Verify every internal and external link.
- [ ] Add analytics only with an appropriate privacy notice and consent approach where required.
- [ ] Test the production domain, HTTPS, 404, and form delivery.
